# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Customer-facing agents: support reps, account managers, customer success managers. This is the first tool they open in the morning — a start-of-day ritual, not a deep-work session. They use it to build and publish modal announcements that their end-users will see. The mood is: fresh, pre-focused, setting up for the day. They need calm clarity, not a busy interface demanding their attention.

## Product Purpose

Release Hub is an announcement builder. Product and customer-facing teams use it to create templated modal announcements (single or multi-section) and publish them to their users. Success looks like: a rep opens the tool, drafts or reviews an announcement, and publishes it in minutes — with zero friction and no UI anxiety.

## Brand Personality

Clean. Professional. Grounding.

Voice: direct and confident, never chatty. The tool does not perform enthusiasm. It shows up ready.

Emotional goal: the interface should feel like opening a well-organized desk in the morning — everything in its place, nothing competing for attention.

## Anti-references

- **Intercom**: bubble nav, badge clutter, enterprise-blue saturation, feature-noise sidebars, over-rounded pill everything. This is the primary anti-reference.
- Overly playful SaaS dashboards (confetti everywhere, emoji-heavy copy, "You're crushing it!" microcopy)
- Dark-mode-as-default tools that feel more like dev tooling than a content builder

## Design Principles

1. **Earn the morning** — the tool is the first thing opened. Every element must justify its presence. Clutter is disrespect of the user's time and focus.
2. **Calm before caffeine** — no alarming colors, no visual noise. The UI should lower heart rate, not raise it.
3. **Professional warmth** — not cold or clinical; not cute or playful. Confident and approachable, like a well-run newsroom.
4. **Show, don't explain** — the preview is the truth. Labels and descriptions are secondary to what the user can see directly.
5. **Zero friction publishing** — every interaction on the critical path (write → preview → publish) should feel inevitable, not effortful.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. No color-only status signals.
